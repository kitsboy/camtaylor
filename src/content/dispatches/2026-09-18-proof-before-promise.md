Every deal starts the same way for me: I go and look at the thing itself before anyone
pitches me on it. The cap table, the contracts, the operating account, the actual
customer list. Boring, unglamorous, and it is the only reason a founder has ever
trusted me with the hard conversation later.

**The order matters.** Most people structure first and verify second, because
structuring is the fun part and it makes you feel useful. I do it backwards. If the
proof survives the read, we design the deal around what is actually true instead of
around what sounded good in a deck.

That is the whole promise of this practice, and it is why the proof dashboard on this
site reports what it can actually reach rather than a score. If a link is down, it says
down. If a reading is unavailable, it says unavailable.

I would rather be the person who says *I could not verify that* than the person whose
deck is beautiful and wrong.
